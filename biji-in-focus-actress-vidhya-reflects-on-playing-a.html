'Biji' in Focus: Actress Vidhya Reflects on Playing a Layered Antagonist, Her Emotional Personal Journey, and Life Beyond the Camera

From facing audience wrath for her portrayal of an overbearing mother to recalling her grueling separation from her infant in Dubai, Vidhya shares an exhaustive account of her career, co-stars, and artistic dreams.

In an expansive and candid discussion, actress Vidhya has opened up about her striking performance as Biji—a character who frustrated audiences with her intense, antagonist-driven motherly persona while others in the film provided comic relief. Recalling her journey into the project, Vidhya revealed that when director Gireesh initially called her, she had little idea of the character's depth or the pervasive negative shades it possessed throughout the narrative. Expressing overwhelming excitement and gratitude for being part of a box-office record-breaking film with a full-length role, she shared the immense satisfaction of contributing to a major cinematic success.

Vidhya candidly recounted her high-strung initial days on set, starting right from the auspicious 'puja' day.

"It was a very tense situation for me. We arrived on the morning of the puja, and by afternoon, they had scheduled our first shot—the complex dining table sequence where I had a mountain of dialogues starting with 'Ennathaa Thankachaa paripadi?' (What is the plan, Thankachan?). I had to start scolding everyone from the very beginning about not going to work right after shifting into the new home,"
Vidhya recalled, emphasizing how daunting it was to step into such heavy scenes before even getting fully acquainted with co-stars like Mamitha Baiju.

From 'Biji' to Set Dynamics: Crafting a Complex Antagonist

Explaining her personal approach to acting, Vidhya noted that she naturally prefers a cheerful and lively persona on set regardless of personal troubles. However, playing Biji required strict adherence to director Gireesh's precise instructions. Whenever her energy dipped, Gireesh would promptly instruct her, "Chechi, let the meter go up a bit higher," guiding her performance step by step. She specifically highlighted a scene where she walked alongside co-stars Nivin Pauly and Sangeeth to get into a car, unaware of the secret relationship between Nivin's and Mamitha's characters.

"I initially stood there looking very serious as I prepared to enter the car. But Gireesh corrected me, saying my character wasn't supposed to know anything about their secret romance yet, so I could just smile normally. I made sure to strictly follow his guidance, because if I had tried adding my own improvisations, things might have gone wrong,"
she explained.

Because comedic sequences were shot separately, Vidhya revealed that she didn't share screen space with the rest of the cast for most of the humor-filled scenes, except for collective sequences like the Goa trip and the church procession (palli pradakshinam). Consequently, she experienced the comedy scenes for the first time sitting in a movie theater alongside general audiences, laughing just like everyone else despite knowing the script.

On-Set Experiences, Palakkad Heat, and Working with Co-Stars

Sharing detailed observations of her co-stars, Vidhya spoke glowingly about Mamitha Baiju's grounded nature and dedication. Recalling a grueling shoot inside a train during Palakkad's intense heat, co-star Vinay Forrt had pointed out Mamitha sleeping on a train berth near a fan despite feeling unwell. Vinay had jokingly asked her, "Is this where a pan-Indian star sleeps?" Vidhya praised Mamitha for being completely devoid of stardom airs, noting how she would sit in a corner of the set for hours after a shot rather than retiring to her caravan. During an extended 10 to 11-day night schedule for the climax fight sequence in a ground, Mamitha generously offered her nearby caravan to Vidhya and child actor Abhiram (who played Allan), creating a warm, supportive atmosphere.

Vidhya also praised Nivin Pauly's unique screen presence, highlighting his spontaneous facial expressions and eye movements, calling his acting style extremely cute and distinct. Her own children were hugely excited about her sharing screen space with Nivin, even insisting she take a smiling photograph with him after her first picture showed him with a serious expression. Furthermore, Vidhya expressed deep admiration for co-star Vinay Forrt's high-energy performance, particularly recalling his hilarious scene running out from behind a stage dressed as a beggar, which left the cast laughing on set.

Social Stigma, Audience Reactions, and Personal Philosophy

Reflecting on audience reactions to her role as Biji, Vidhya admitted receiving a flood of negative and critical messages on social media following the film's release.

"Initially, seeing all those negative comments made me feel a bit sad. But then I realized it meant the audience found the character relatable and accepted the performance,"
she stated. Viewers sent humorous messages asking what was wrong with character Justin or why the mother couldn't marry off Ashley. People even left comments under her personal photos with Nivin Pauly, lightheartedly accusing her character of posing for pictures after creating hurdles for Ashley's marriage.

Addressing broader societal topics raised by film discussions, such as age-gap relationships, Vidhya acknowledged that social stigma remains strong in local society compared to international cultures or film industries abroad where age differences are normalized. On the topic of emotional commitment between parents and children, she emphasized that mutual respect and basic trust are vital in any relationship, though she strongly condemned the extreme emotional blackmail exhibited by her character Biji.

"I grew up in a household with a very strict father and a homemaker mother who wasn't given the space to express opinions. I had to build my own strength to voice my choices when marriage came up, largely because I was working in media at the time. Because of that background, I pledged never to impose choices on my own children. I give them complete space and communicate openly,"
Vidhya shared.

A Mother’s Heartbreaking Past: From Dubai to Real Estate

Vidhya shared a deeply emotional story from her early adult life that shaped her resilience. After starting her career in a private media channel where she worked for five years, she married and later relocated to Dubai following her husband's radio job offer. However, due to an urgent financial issue involving a bank, her husband needed to return to Kerala while she was required to urgently take up a position at a Middle East television channel office in Dubai.

At the time, Vidhya was a young mother who had to leave behind her firstborn baby girl—then just three to four months old—expecting to return within a week. Due to employment requirements, she ended up staying in Dubai for a full year before she could see her infant again.

"I went into that Middle East channel office with tear-stained eyes. That pain of leaving my infant child behind is something I will never forget until the day I die. Whenever my eldest daughter faces any hardship today, I still recall that period with deep sorrow,"
she revealed. Following her return to Kerala a year later, Vidhya stepped away from media work for over a decade, building a successful career in administration—working as an HR professional, a Patient Relations Officer at a hospital, and spending 12 years as the Head of Customer Relations in the real estate sector.

Re-entry into Cinema, Dream Roles, and a Special Lullaby

Vidhya's entry into feature films happened unexpectedly when she accompanied her younger daughter, Dhathri, to the sets of director Jis Joy's film Vijay Superum Pournamiyum. Jis Joy unexpectedly invited Vidhya to act onscreen alongside her real-life husband, Sanal, while their children Dhwani and Dhathri also appeared in the film—marking a rare instance of an entire family acting together in a single production. Encouraged post-pandemic by her daughters, Vidhya began submitting audition tapes, leading to prominent roles in projects such as Kerala Crime Files (playing Indrans' resolute wife) and sitcom guest appearances on Thatteem Mutteem.

She also recounted a memorable appearance in a major Mohanlal film, where she had initially hoped to play a wedding host welcoming Mohanlal's character, only to discover on set that her character was a complete stranger watching guests arrive. Despite her original scene being edited down, watching Mohanlal's natural acting mastery up close remains an unforgettable learning moment for her.

Looking ahead, Vidhya expressed strong desires to work with veteran directors like Sathyan Anthikad and Sibi Malayil, as well as acting alongside megastar Mammootty, having grown up watching his films in Shastri Nagar, Thiruvananthapuram. Her dream roles include playing a school teacher, principal, doctor, lawyer, a police officer featuring in action sequences, and mentally complex characters similar to K.P.A.C. Lalitha's classic portrayals.

Concluding the interaction on a heartfelt note, Vidhya sang a soothing traditional Malayalam lullaby live during the interview: "Paattu paadi urakkam njan thamarappoombaithale... keettu keettu nee uranggen karalinte kaathale..." She dedicated the song to her children, particularly her younger daughter, as a tender tribute to the comforting moments she missed offering her eldest child during those grueling months apart years ago.
