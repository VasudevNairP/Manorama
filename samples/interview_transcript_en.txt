Q: Dr. Menon, could you tell us about the new solar energy project your team is deploying?
Dr. Menon: Yes, we are rolling out a 50-megawatt distributed floating solar installation on several reservoir networks. Phase one is expected to generate 70 gigawatt-hours annually.
Q: What makes this approach unique compared to standard ground-mounted panels?
Dr. Menon: Standard panels take up valuable agricultural land. By placing bifacial photovoltaic modules on water reservoirs, we reduce water evaporation by nearly 35 percent and the water cooling effect actually improves panel efficiency by 12 percent. As I always say, "Engineering should work in harmony with nature, not compete with it."
Q: How much did the project cost?
Dr. Menon: The initial capital outlay for phase one is around 180 crore rupees, funded through a public-private partnership.
Q: Are there any environmental concerns from local fishermen?
Dr. Menon: A few groups raised questions about sunlight penetration impacting aquatic plants. We conducted a 6-month limnological assessment and found that covering only 20 percent of the reservoir surface area keeps the aquatic ecosystem completely healthy.
